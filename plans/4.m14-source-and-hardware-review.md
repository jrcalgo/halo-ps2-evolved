## PS2 resource and throughput analysis

### Memory: facts versus budget assumptions

**Verified architectural figures stated by the PS2 submodule design notes:** EE has 32 MiB RDRAM, 294.912 MHz in-order R5900, 16 KiB scratchpad, and no L2 (`halo-ps2/docs/01-ps2-architecture.md:31-48`). IOP is R3000A at 36.9 MHz with 2 MiB RAM; GS has 4 MiB eDRAM; SPU2 has 48 voices and 2 MiB sound RAM (`01-ps2-architecture.md:95-113,145-160`). These are hardware constraints, not measured current-build allocations.

**Active budget, explicitly not full M14 allocation evidence:** parent `config/memory-budget.json` version 2 commits 27,787,264 bytes (26.5 MiB) and reserves 5,767,168 bytes (5.5 MiB), totaling exactly 33,554,432 EE bytes. Its regions are system/kernel 1,572,864; ELF/static 2,097,152; resident gameplay 6,815,744; BSP 6,291,456; texture cache 4,194,304; dynamic simulation 2,621,440; DMA 1,179,648; streaming 1,572,864; checkpoint/audio 524,288 each; frame scratch 393,216. The older `halo-ps2/docs/02-specs.md:111-129` split is historical design guidance, not the active account. M14 cannot assume that its reserve is available or that every region has already achieved its cap.

`docs/memory.md` records a stream diagnostic requesting a 12,582,912-byte arena, using 5,279,024 after load and reaching historical high water 6,465,536; later entries assign section bytes and OTEX load scratch across regions. `halo-ps2/engine/src/main.c:137-140` defaults to a 20 MiB arena when configuration does not override it. Reserved capacity, actual usage, regional accounting and transient high water are distinct. M14 still needs the complete active play ELF map, allocator accounts, pose/HUD/effect pools, stacks and simultaneous EE/IOP/SPU upload ownership. Do not add BSS staging into both ELF and streaming accounts or equate compressed file size with resident bytes. No full M14 peak-overlap measurement is available.

SPU2 plan allocation is 1.5 MiB banks + 256 KiB stream ring + ~200 KiB reverb + remainder/system (`02-specs.md:128-129`). IOP plan is ~700 KiB modules plus ~1 MiB read-ahead (`02-specs.md:128-129`), which leaves little room for duplicated decode staging and SIF RPC buffers. Proposed implication: convert/decode offline; runtime IOP does I/O, bounded ring refill and SPU2 voice/DMA management, not general asset decode. Verify exact linker/IRX footprints and peak simultaneous staging.

GS budget: chosen 512x448 targets reserve two PSMCT16S buffers of 448 KiB each, Z24 stored in 896 KiB, leaving about 2.25 MiB for textures/CLUTs (`01-ps2-architecture.md:133-143`). Halo texture data has no DXT on GS; use paletted PSMT8/4 or selective PSMCT16, texture streaming over PATH3. One texture per primitive/pass; multi-texture effects require extra passes (`01-ps2-architecture.md:97-120`). Plan figures include 4 MiB GS total and M14 texture target/share (`02-specs.md:105-110,143-145`). HUD atlases, weapon skins, particles, decals and current BSP textures compete for this same pool. Maintain explicit residency leases/eviction and cap upload bytes per frame; the plan target is <=1.5 MiB texture upload/frame, not a demonstrated safe sustained rate (`02-specs.md:105-110`).

### CPU, tick, VU, DMA and graphics contention

At 30 Hz each frame is a 33.3 ms wall-clock interval. The plan allocates EE simulation <=12 ms, render prep <=8 ms, audio/pad/SIF <=1.5 ms, stream/decompress/bank uploads <=5 ms, checkpoint <=2 ms, and reserve <=4.8 ms; VU1 and GS are parallel paths targeted <=28 ms (`02-specs.md:78-99`). These are targets, not profiling results. M14 must report worst and percentile EE phase cost under four-player combat; do not charge audio decode/upload as “free” just because it runs on IOP or DMA, because it contends for buses and interrupts. A bounded audio-event queue can prevent variable work in simulation; fixed cost per tick needs profiling.

VU1 has 16 KiB micro/data memory and receives geometry via VIF1 UNPACK / MSCAL; GIF PATH3 can upload textures alongside PATH1 VU1 drawing (`01-ps2-architecture.md:59-86`). Skinning and viewmodel expansion therefore need small batches fitting VU memory; do not send unbounded per-particle work. M14 target <=400 VU batches/frame, <=8 skinned LOD0 characters and <=512 particle sprites is in `02-specs.md:101-110`; these must be validated together with weapon viewmodels and player models. VU occupancy, GS fill, and PATH3 upload overlap are not independent budgets—measure collision/late packets on hardware.

DMA transfers are quadword-oriented and use VIF/GIF/SIF paths (`01-ps2-architecture.md:73-86`). Keep IOP RPC messages small and fixed, use aligned staging, track DMA completion before buffer reuse, and never wait synchronously in the simulation tick. Texture uploads use PATH3; SPU2 transfers use its audio DMA/voice-transfer path, which can compete with DVD→IOP→SIF traffic. Failure and timeout paths must discard/retire presentation work only.

### Audio encoding, streaming and voices

Halo CE source sound codecs include uncompressed, Xbox ADPCM, IMA ADPCM and Ogg (`halo-ce-universal/source/sound/sound_manager.c:305-310`). The PS2 design assumes primarily Xbox ADPCM input and targets PS2 ADPCM at 22.05 kHz mono, stereo reserved for music, with dialogue/music streaming (`halo-ps2/docs/02-specs.md:143-149`; tag inventory examples in `halo-ps2/docs/reports/tag-inventory.md:102-104`). These must not be conflated: Xbox ADPCM blocks are not SPU2 VAG blocks. Conversion must decode source formats (including Ogg if actual selected tags use it), resample/downmix under an explicit channel policy, then encode SPU2 ADPCM. The PS2SDK `tools/ps2adpcm` tool is described as PCM→ADPCM in `README`; it is not evidence of Xbox/Ogg decode support. Validate frame counts, loop start/end alignment, predictor state, channel order, clipping and audible/reference error by decoding the generated VAG back to PCM.

PS2SDK offers `sceSdInit` and `sceSdVoiceTrans` declarations (`ps2sdk/common/include/libsd-common.h:157,183`); audsrv waits SPU DMA status (`iop/sound/audsrv/src/adpcm.c:200-201`). This is a transfer mechanism, not a complete production driver. Manage both cores explicitly if all 48 physical voices are required. Preserve source class priority/distance and instance rules; any different UI/dialogue reservation, priority ordering or tie-break is an explicit PS2 policy requiring tests, not source parity by assumption. Stereo voice ADPCM and crossfades consume multiple physical voices. Voice stealing must stop the old loop, release owner tokens and increment generation so stale commands cannot kill the replacement.

For streaming, use fixed rings with watermarks, priority reads and measured DVD/SIF/IOP service time. Test bank page-in coincident with BSP/texture reads and combat. The IOP decompression estimate (5–10 MB/s) is explicitly a plan estimate (`01-ps2-architecture.md:145-152`). PCSX2 results do not certify physical DMA/cache/GS timing. The governing M14 acceptance asks for no underruns in the configured test; label emulator evidence accordingly and carry physical-console qualification as separate outstanding evidence.

### Authoritative effects must survive cosmetic failure

CE `effects/effects.c:1584-1795` uses the same effect dispatcher for cosmetic parts and `object_new` / `area_of_effect_cause_damage`. The object path consumes the global gameplay random stream; particle/decal branches use local randomness. Allocation at `:1491-1553` can evict a nondeterministic effect to admit a deterministic one and rejects deterministic children from particle-only paths. Dropping a complete `effe` event as if it were cosmetic can change combat.

Classify dependencies offline; unresolved/mixed definitions must not silently enter a lossy pool. M13 executes authoritative branches in source order and retains RNG semantics. A one-way bounded cosmetic/audio queue carries tick, generation-stamped source/owner/event IDs, marker/transform, variant seed and references. Overflow records diagnostics without changing simulation. No authoritative damage/object event goes into that lossy queue. Audio and animation-marker IDs are consumed once across redraws and multi-tick frames; loops stop on switch/death/destruction/map unload.

CE particles resolve FP/object attachment before location (`particles.c:810-860`); decals include projection/clipping and BSP lifetime (`decals.c:985-1557`). `decal_insert:2450-2525` has lock/unlock exhaustion/error behavior, not generic oldest-first replacement. Define a PS2 cosmetic policy explicitly rather than asserting it reproduces all source pool behavior.

### IOP/SPU allocator, transfer and underrun hazards

`ps2sdk/ee/rpc/audsrv/src/audsrv_rpc.c:431-479` allocates an IOP buffer the size of the whole sample, SIF-DMAs into it with busy polling, synchronously calls RPC, then frees it. EE staging, full IOP staging and destination SPU storage coexist. A whole-bank convenience upload is therefore unsafe as a capacity assumption on 2 MiB IOP RAM. Prefer fixed upload chunks, a bounded command queue and completion fences.

`iop/sound/audsrv/src/adpcm.c:160-201` appends SPU data after the tail starting at 0x5010, with a TODO about PCM streaming overlap and a 2,097,152-byte upper bound. `free_sample:81-120` frees descriptors with documented tail-reclamation limitations, not arbitrary bank compaction. Descriptors allocate IOP heap; allocation failure and arithmetic require review before production use. `:235-305` inspects ENDX on core 1 only; automatic allocation scans channels 1–23, omitting channel 0. Do not infer 48 usable voices or complete bank lifetime management from this sample.

`iop/sound/libsd/src/voice.c:33-66` checks DMA-in-progress and rounds transfer lengths in 64-byte units; `:150` exposes status. Bank format/driver must enforce padded extent bounds, alignment/cache writeback and ownership until both SIF and SPU completion. Unload key-offs/drains voices and DMA before reclamation. Generation IDs prevent stale stops/completions from freeing reused slots; ENDX alone cannot represent active looping-stream ownership.
