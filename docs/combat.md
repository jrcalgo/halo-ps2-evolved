# M13 combat implementation record

**Status: incomplete, in progress (2026-10-19).** The governing acceptance criteria are in [M13 of the plan](../plans/0.native-ps2-halo-hybrid-port.md#16-milestone-m13--implement-weapons-projectiles-and-damage).

The first checkpoint adds `src/game/combat.{h,c}`, a bounded, fixed-tick combat state machine. It has four player slots, two carried weapon slots each, 16 targets, 16 pickups, 32 projectiles, and 32 presentation events per player. The host fixture checks pickup, equip, semi-automatic and automatic fire, magazine depletion, reload, shield spill into health, death, melee, grenade throw, explosion, projectile-pool saturation, and equal results when presentation work occurs between identical simulation ticks. A grenade uses a separate explosive definition, independent of the held weapon. The C source has no heap allocation.

The original `combat.{h,c}` checkpoint remains a **synthetic prototype**, not a port of Halo's weapon semantics. Its transition-fixture values are not retail constants, its target array is not the M12 player object system, and it cannot close M13 or serve as the production gun implementation. Separate modules now use retail weapon/projectile data for host tests, and the play EE loop's projectile-to-vitality path has been visibly exercised in PCSX2 interpreter and recompiler modes against a fixed diagnostic sphere. The sphere is not a retail actor; this limited smoke does not establish actor integration or all-weapon behavior. The pinned Halo CE source is authoritative for one-for-one behavior. PS2 work must preserve those outcomes while using the PS2 submodule's renderer, pack, memory, and platform boundaries. A clean-room substitute is acceptable only after equivalence is demonstrated against the source and reference scenarios.

## Source audit and full weapon scope

The pinned `halo-64` source has `source/items/weapons.c`, `weapon_definitions.h`, `projectiles.c`, and `source/objects/damage.c`. The original reload path uses `rounds_total` and `rounds_loaded` and derives reload duration from a first-person animation; some weapons can reload again for another increment. The firing path resolves trigger, magazine, projectile and damage-effect tags. The current prototype instead uses a simple reserve count and a fixed reload tick field. Those differences must be reconciled from tags and source before reference matching.

The locally supplied decompressed Xbox Blood Gulch map indexes these `weap` tags:

| Group | Weapon tags / required behavior |
|---|---|
| Human handheld | assault rifle, pistol, shotgun, rocket launcher, sniper rifle, flamethrower; pellet count/spread and shotgun per-shell reload need distinct state |
| Covenant handheld | plasma rifle, plasma pistol, needler, gravity rifle; heat/overheat, charge, tracking and supercombine need distinct state |
| Vehicle-mounted | Warthog gun, Scorpion cannon, Ghost gun; mount ownership and projectile/damage paths need coverage |
| Objective items | ball and flag; pickup/carry/drop/melee interactions need coverage, though they are not ordinary guns |
| Grenades | frag and plasma `eqip`/`proj` pairs; fuse, bounce, attachment and blast behavior need coverage |

The map inventory now covers all 24 `.map` files in the user-supplied Xbox disc, extracted through the pinned bounded XDVDFS reader into ignored `data/retail/maps/` and decompressed under ignored `data/retail/weapon-audit-maps/`. It finds **23 distinct `weap` paths: 20 with trigger definitions and three without (energy sword, flag, ball)**. The energy sword is a melee weapon and must still receive source-equivalent behavior. Beyond the Blood Gulch set, those paths include Banshee gun, Wraith mortar, C-gun turret, dropship turret, Sentinel weapon, energy sword, fuel rod, and Hunter fuel rod. Frag and plasma grenade equipment and projectile tags are inventoried separately. The 24-map report is ignored `build/combat-m13/all-map-weapons.json`; the input maps and report are never staged. `src/game/arsenal.c` runs all 23 paths from those tag fields. A 90-tick hold matches the shared rules: the pistol fires every 9 ticks, the rifle every 2, the shotgun on ticks 0, 30, and 60, the rocket and sniper once because they are latched, the gravity rifle and the Sentinel every tick, and the fuel rods once at tick 38 after the 1.25-second charge. The plasma pistol is released at tick 40 and fires then. The needler accelerates from 3 shots per second and stops at 20 rounds. The energy sword does not fire; its melee effect is 151. The flag and the ball do not fire. The play EE camera-origin path now applies tag-driven cone error plus CE `projectile_distribute` offsets; the fixed-seed shotgun cone and the fan equations have host tests. This is not yet complete per-weapon projectile/guidance/damage parity. First-person reload frames are only verified for pistol, rifle, and shotgun. Vehicle weapons, objective items, and grenades need separate command and ownership paths. Portability of original source should be tested in a narrow R5900 compile closure before replacing those behaviors with approximations.

`tools/audit_weapons.py` now reads the verified 32-bit magazine and trigger layouts through the pinned `halo-ps2` tag index. It bounds-checks reflexives, links projectile references by tag ID, inventories grenade equipment/projectile tags, and writes only an ignored report. Reproduce the full report with `python3 tools/audit_weapons.py data/retail/weapon-audit-maps/*.map --output build/combat-m13/all-map-weapons.json`. Blood Gulch has 15 weapon tags: 13 firing weapons plus ball and flag. The audit finds the source tag fields for pistol/assault-rifle magazines, shotgun's 15 projectiles per shot, the plasma pistol's two triggers and 0.6-second charge field, and Scorpion's two triggers. The tag field `reload_seconds_tag` is **not** yet the authoritative runtime reload duration: the original code uses first-person animation time. The tag audit is a conversion input, not weapon-behavior proof.

## Original-source R5900 compile probe

`python3 tools/probe_combat_source.py` cross-compiles **unchanged** pinned `weapons.c`, `projectiles.c`, and `damage.c` into R5900 objects with GCC 15.2.0. The source probe generates a compatibility prefix under ignored `build/combat-source-probe/` and uses the N64 capacity header solely to satisfy compile-time declarations. It does not use N64 capacities for any production PS2 runtime. The three objects have 79, 112, and 125 unresolved symbols respectively before linking. The probe now generates the pinned port's MSVC inline-semantics header, then makes a **relocatable partial link** with function/data sections and `weapon_update`, `projectile_update`, and `object_cause_damage` as retained roots. This resolves duplicate inline definitions and produces `combat-core.o`. Its symbol table has 180 undefined entries, but only **118 have surviving relocations**; the retained three-root path has no live Direct3D relocation. Raw source objects do expose D3D references, so expanding the roots requires the same check again. `halo_random` resolves weakly in this probe, which is not a substitute for a production RNG contract. The exact live external list, source/object hashes, generated-header hash, and build logs are in private `build/combat-source-probe/result.json` and `live-externals.txt`.

The 118 live dependencies split the next integration work into source-backed **simulation services** (object lifecycle and datum pools, collision, units, AI decisions, game rules, projectile spawning, animation), **tag and world data** (`tag_get`, `tag_block_get_element_with_size`, scenario, globals), and **PS2 boundary services** (time, effects, sound, network presentation, profiling, diagnostics). Those are planning groups, not a claim that stubbing any one service preserves the game. In particular, tag lookup, object state, collision and damage must operate on the same gameplay records as the retained source. Visual/audio effects may be adapted only after their callbacks are shown not to change simulation results. Additional entry roots for construction, pickup, equipment, weapon switching, and every special weapon path still need closure analysis; the current three-root link is not a final EE executable and has never executed.

`python3 tools/probe_combat_source.py --foundations` adds unchanged pinned `source/memory/data.c` and `source/game/game_time.c` to a separate partial link. Both compile; `datum_get` and `game_time_get` resolve, leaving **116** live externals and introducing none in this retained path. This is a useful source-closure result, but the original datum and tick implementations still need initialization, PS2 capacity decisions, and an interface to the converted HP2 data. The expanded report lives in ignored `build/combat-source-foundations/result.json`.

`python3 tools/probe_combat_source.py --foundations --lifecycle` retains **17** original entry points: the three update/damage roots plus weapon creation/deletion/ownership, inventory handling, placement, ready/put-away and melee, projectile creation/deletion, vitality initialization/update, area damage, and damage-data construction. The five unchanged source files still partially link. All 17 roots are present in the result; **125** externals have live relocations, nine more than the smaller five-file graph, with no live D3D relocation. The new dependencies include equipment pickup, world-object sphere queries, shield HUD feedback, sound/effect dispatch, network-game state, and breakable-surface area damage. Their exact symbols and object hash are in ignored `build/combat-source-foundations-lifecycle/result.json`. This broader set still omits other combat entry points and must not be read as coverage of all 23 retail weapons.

The next unchanged-source layer is reproducible with `--leaf-support`. It adds `real_math.c`, `random_math.c`, `periodic_functions.c`, and `tag_groups.c`. Those four files resolve 22 externals and introduce four toolchain/math calls, reducing the graph from 125 to 107 live externals. The probe reports compiler/libc/libm symbols separately from game/platform symbols so soft-double helper calls are not mistaken for missing Halo services.

Exact native-port trigonometry matters to random spread and projectile aiming. `--deterministic-math` therefore compiles all 26 pinned `port/third_party/musl-math` sources with the same no-contraction policy described by that port. It resolves `halo_sin`, `halo_cos`, and `halo_acos`. The resulting 17-root graph has **115** live externals: **19 toolchain runtime symbols** and **96 game/platform symbols**, with no live D3D relocation. The larger raw count is expected because R5900 lowers double-precision work to libgcc helpers and the musl code calls exact libc/libm primitives. Those runtime symbols still require proof in a final PS2SDK link. This step preserves the pinned port's deterministic math boundary; it does not substitute host `libm` behavior.

`src/game/gun.c` follows `weapon_trigger_can_fire_again` for a non-analog trigger with no age penalty, then the magazine and inventory transitions those two weapons actually take. Blood Gulch gives the assault rifle 15 shots per second, 60 rounds loaded, and 240 carried, and the pistol 3.5 shots per second, 12 loaded, and 60 carried. Holding an already-ready trigger for 30 ticks discharges 15 rifle rounds on the even ticks and pistol rounds at ticks 0, 9, 18, and 27.

The runtime reload length is the first-person reload-empty clip: 87 frames for the rifle and 67 for the pistol. The tag's reload seconds are 3.4 and 2.17 and are not the timer. Both magazines have chamber time 0. An empty magazine arms the reload on the tick after the last shot, because that check runs after the magazine timer. Rounds return `frame count` ticks later, and the next shot is two ticks after that, once the unchambered magazine spends a tick chambering. A player-owned reload removes only the rounds that enter the magazine, leaving the rifle 120 in reserve and the pistol 36.

Pickup fills the first free slot of four and leaves a second weapon stowed. `units_update` readies the desired weapon before `objects_update` decrements its ready timer, so the first shot is on tick 28 for the rifle (29 ready frames) and tick 34 for the pistol (35 ready frames). Switching after the rifle's first shot readies the pistol on the next tick and fires it 34 ticks later. A fifth pickup is refused. Neither weapon changes weapon state for ready or reload: the rifle's object animation reference is empty, and the pistol's ready and reload slots are empty, so `weapon_set_state` does not leave idle.

The shotgun is weapon type 1. It fires once per second, 15 pellets and one shell each time, and must chamber after every shot that leaves a shell. That chamber fits inside the 30-tick gap, so a ready shotgun fires on ticks 0 and 30. A reload inserts one shell at a time. The first shell of a reload uses the 15-frame enter animation; each shell after that uses the 12-frame reload-empty animation. The exit animations are read by the source and then ignored. Holding the trigger loads one shell and fires it on tick 16. Reloading without the trigger held chains shells, so 12 shells finish on tick 134 and the reserve is empty. Pellet spread is not applied yet. `python3 -B -m unittest discover -s tests -p test_gun.py -v` checks the pistol, rifle, and shotgun tag fields and these tick results.

`src/game/shot.c` is the bullet those two guns discharge. Blood Gulch stores the pistol bullet at just over 10 units per tick and the rifle bullet at 10.8, both with air gravity scale 1 and a maximum range of 40. One open tick moves by the tag velocity and drops half of `global_gravity` (0.0035651792). The fourth tick reaches the range and detonates. A surface hit reads `material_index` at byte 10 of the collision surface. Material 0 disappears, material 9 overpenetrates and therefore detonates because the timer upper bound is 0, and material 32 reflects. Every response has zero noise, but each collision still draws the two noise values from the 32-bit LCG (`seed * 1664525 + 1013904223`). On object hit, CE scales impact damage from current, initial, and final projectile speed, then interpolates between the damage effect's minimum and random bounds. The pack carries the minimum and `shot_tick` applies that formula; a decelerating-impact fixture verifies the 50%-speed case while preserving the random draw. CE's within-tick crossing of final velocity is also reproduced: it calculates the crossing fraction, uses the 0.99 final-speed factor, and integrates average velocity over the remainder of that tick. A synthetic crossing trace checks resulting position and velocity. A sphere of radius 0.5 at 5 units is struck at 4.5. `python3 -B -m unittest discover -s tests -p test_shot.py -v` checks the tag bits and these results.

The primary and trigger projectile packs now preserve all CE per-material potential-response fields in `POT1`/`POT2`. `shot_tick` consumes the CE impact-speed and impact-angle noise draws, applies source short-circuit angle/velocity/unit-only gates, then consumes the skip roll only when those gates pass. A unit-only potential-response fixture distinguishes an ordinary object from a unit target. This is not yet a retail actor result: EE target classification, converted collision-material mapping, potential/default effect dispatch and their damage/audio semantics are still absent. The current all-weapon source/tag crosswalk and per-family material records are in [`m13-step10-fuel-rod-flight-investigation.md`](m13-step10-fuel-rod-flight-investigation.md).

`src/game/vital.c` applies that damage to a bounded cyborg probe. Shield and body maxima are both 75, stored normalized the way the object datum stores them. This path uses the collision tag's indirect damage material record 1 (material type 21): zero shield leak, unit shield/body multipliers; the damage effect's shield-material type is 22. One pistol hit leaves the shield at two thirds. Three pistol hits empty it, and the fifth kills because the overflow reaches the body and the pistol's material-21 modifier is 1.5. The rifle's modifier is 1, so its eighth hit is the first to reach the body and leaves the body at 14/15. A pistol headshot does not kill through a full shield, because no damage amount reaches the body. With the shield already empty it sets the body to zero and then applies the overflow, which kills. The collision tag sets minimum shield-stun damage to 6 and stun time to 4 seconds (120 ticks at 30 Hz): a 5-point shield hit does not stun, while a hit that depletes the shield always resets stun. The map stores the recharge step as `0x3c088889` (1/120 of the shield per tick). Recharge begins on the tick after the stun reaches zero. CE chooses `body_damage_multiplier` from the collision model's selected damage-material record; the sphere probe has no collision-region attribution and therefore uses its indirect record's unit multiplier for every hit (not a synthetic leg multiplier). Headshot classification is likewise supplied to the helper rather than derived from a rendered actor collision. `python3 -B -m unittest discover -s tests -p test_vital.py -v` checks the tag fields and these bounded results.

The cyborg starts with four frag grenades. `characters\cyborg\melee` is 1000 damage, so one melee empties the shield and kills. A throw sets the grenade's velocity to 10/30 along the aim and draws the fuse length immediately. Frag and plasma fuses are 0.5 s and 2 s, but `projectile_update` does not run that timer until the grenade is at rest. In float32 the frag timer reaches 1 on resting tick 15, and the plasma timer reaches 1 on resting tick 61. The explosion effect is 80 minimum and 120 to 120. Inside the falloff (1.5 for frag, 0.5 for plasma) that is 120, which leaves the body at 0.4. A second blast kills. At the frag cutoff of 2.5 the scale is 0 and nothing is damaged. Halfway between the frag falloff and cutoff the blend is 100. The shock-wave effect is 0 damage. On material 0, both grenades bounce. Frag friction keeps 10 percent of the sideways speed and 20 percent of the impact speed; plasma keeps 5 percent and 10 percent. Thrown across a floor from one unit up, the frag stops on tick 34 after 2 bounces and the plasma stops on tick 29. The fuse then runs: 15 ticks for frag and 61 for plasma. Plasma's stick response is limited to units, so the same floor does not stick it, and a unit surface sticks on the first hit. Each bounce draws the two zero-width noise values. A unit stick also draws the potential-response random. A bounded host stress trace now rests eight frag grenades, advances all on a shared fuse schedule, and processes all eight explosions against one cyborg profile in stable slot order; subsequent explosions still consume blast RNG after target death. This exercises simultaneous-event pressure in the grenade/vitality modules, but does not prove retail global object-update ordering or EE grenade integration. `python3 -B -m unittest discover -s tests -p test_grenade.py -v` checks the tag fields and these results.
