# M2 Step 8 independent review: capacity blocker and next safe experiment

**Disposition:** The whole-image campaign layout is disproven by a source-backed lower bound, not by a failed runtime attempt. No implementation-ready all-campaign EE topology is currently evidenced. The next *safe implementation slice* is an opt-in, no-placement-change observer for CE data access and owner lifetimes, with exact coverage tests and a resident-data oracle. Treat its results as diagnostics only. Do not create a lease/cache, resize an arena, open campaign catalog admission, or count any candidate bytes as saved until the observer has measured a representative workload and a separate complete owner/lifetime design passes parity.

This is a report-only review. `plans/5.hcu-remake.md` remains authoritative for all-map scope, full campaign AI/scripts/effects, PS2 hardware constraints, and unchanged reserve; `plans/7.hcu-remake-m2-m4.md` remains the execution plan. In particular, Step 8 says the existing bind-order/lifecycle evidence is not sufficient and keeps campaign admission closed pending a lawful topology and target high-water. This review does not close any M2 gate.

## What is established, and what is not

### Measured/statistically reproducible facts

* Budget v2 commits 27,787,264 of 33,554,432 EE bytes and reserves 5,767,168 bytes; `current_bsp` is 6,291,456 bytes, `dynamic_simulation` is 2,621,440 bytes, `resident_gameplay` is 6,815,744 bytes, and the remaining texture/DMA/stream/audio/checkpoint/scratch owners have explicit roles in [`memory-budget.json`](../../config/memory-budget.json#L4-L20). The reserve is not a candidate source.
* Under the current whole-resident representation, the worst campaign pair is compact complete d20 CTAG plus the largest referenced complete raw BSP (`d20_hangar`). After 16-byte span rounding the pair is 18,564,640 bytes. The capacity-derived CE claims extent is `ceil((18,564,640 / 32)) * 4 = 2,320,580` bytes. Adding the exact lower-bound fixed rows from the matching audit—system/kernel 1,572,864; ELF LOAD 2,086,532; ELF BSS 2,114,588; CE game state 3,428,352—gives 30,087,556 bytes. This is 2,300,292 bytes above the *entire* 27,787,264-byte committed ceiling **before** any dynamic simulation, texture/lightmap data, DMA, streaming, audio, scratch, validation/adapter overhead, renderer ownership, or transition overlap. These inputs and calculation are independently tabulated in [`m2-capacity-viable-design-review-2026-10-21.md`](m2-capacity-viable-design-review-2026-10-21.md#L34-L59) and [`m2-2026-10-21-budget-repartition-review.md`](m2-2026-10-21-budget-repartition-review.md#L48-L72).
* Even the impossible sensitivity case “remove every `animation.data` byte at zero EE cache/metadata/staging cost” leaves just 2,751,784 bytes for every other d20 EE owner and any pinned animation. Across campaign rows, the tightest residual is 2,404,132 bytes for a10. This is arithmetic, not a live-set measurement. The 2,621,440-byte dynamic-simulation reservation exceeding either residual is a warning, not proof that all of that nominal capacity must be live at the same instant; no actual worst-phase high-water currently establishes how much can be safely shared.
* A second combined sensitivity test hypothetically removes all animation bytes and all compressed BSP vertex bytes at zero replacement/query/index cost. The retained report says even its best mission (c20) totals 30,527,104 bytes with the other named owners held at current capacities, 2,739,840 bytes above commitment; a30 is 33,178,144 bytes. This rules out that *pair of zero-cost subtractions* as a complete solution, not every future lossless architecture. See [`m2-combined-animation-bsp-capacity-bound-2026-10-21.md`](m2-combined-animation-bsp-capacity-bound-2026-10-21.md#L1-L8) and its reproducible table at [L35-L64](m2-combined-animation-bsp-capacity-bound-2026-10-21.md#L35).
* The PS2 binder allocates one tag cache at the beginning of `current_bsp`, gives the remaining contiguous tail to one active BSP, and charges CE's claims buffer to `resident_gameplay` ([`hcu_memory_bind.c:19-70`](../../src/stream/hcu_memory_bind.c#L19), [`tag_validate.c:248-283`](../../submodules/halo-ce-universal/port/linux/game/tag_validate.c#L248)). The tag+BSP spans are capacity inputs to claims; shrinking a payload without changing the interface does not automatically shrink claims or establish memory savings.
* halo-ps2's streamed HP2 visitor has bounded codec work buffers, then feeds decoded windows to a sink (`section.c:89-148`); its ordinary materializer still reserves the complete decoded output in the destination arena (`section.c:153 onward`). HCU's CBSP stream sink copies decoded raw BSP image bytes into the fixed BSP destination, buffering the envelope and validating that the complete image/relocation table fits there ([`ps2_cache.c:407-465`](../../submodules/halo-ce-universal/port/ps2/src/ps2_cache.c#L407)). This reduces duplicate encoded staging but cannot evade the final complete BSP image requirement.

### Source-backed interface constraints

HCU's CE tag structures embed direct pointers: `tag_block.address` and `tag_data.address` are ordinary pointers, while `tag_block_get_element_fast()` returns `block->address + index * element_size` ([`tag_groups.h:87-119,134-145`](../../submodules/halo-ce-universal/source/tag_files/tag_groups.h#L87), [`tag_groups.c:91-114`](../../submodules/halo-ce-universal/source/tag_files/tag_groups.c#L91)). The central `tag_data_get_pointer()` checks range, then returns `data->address + offset`; it does not consult a backing provider ([`tag_groups.c:91-114`](../../submodules/halo-ce-universal/source/tag_files/tag_groups.c#L91)). Direct `.address` readers also exist, so changing one helper is not a complete paging seam.

The production CE cache path reads the complete tag-data image into the bound tag cache, verifies it and runs tag schema validation before publishing `tag_header` and `global_tag_instances` ([`cache_files.c:1262-1332`](../../submodules/halo-ce-universal/source/cache/cache_files.c#L1262)). The PS2 BSP path similarly relocates a complete CBSP image to the fixed BSP allocation before structure pointers are established ([`ps2_cache.c:407-465`](../../submodules/halo-ce-universal/port/ps2/src/ps2_cache.c#L407)). Scenario BSP switching unloads the previous BSP, loads and verifies one replacement, and publishes `global_structure_bsp`, `global_collision_bsp`, and `global_bsp3d` only after success; failed loads may reload the previous BSP ([`scenario.c:1116-1188`](../../submodules/halo-ce-universal/source/scenario/scenario.c#L1116)). A data eviction policy must therefore respect actual pointer lifetimes, schema validation and rollback, not just file offsets.

`animation.data` is the best *instrumentation candidate*, not a decided optimization. The public frame accessor returns a direct pointer from `animation->data.address` ([`model_animation_definitions.c:500-515`](../../submodules/halo-ce-universal/source/models/model_animation_definitions.c#L500)); compressed rotation/translation/scale helpers each bypass it through direct `tag_data_get_pointer()` calls ([`model_animations.c:2145-2153`](../../submodules/halo-ce-universal/source/models/model_animations.c#L2145), [L2289-L2298](../../submodules/halo-ce-universal/source/models/model_animations.c#L2289), [L2424-L2432](../../submodules/halo-ce-universal/source/models/model_animations.c#L2424)). Before publication, `animation_data_problem()` directly dereferences the compressed payload and validates its tables; CE's correction changes malformed animations to a no-node state ([`tag_schema_models.c:862-955`](../../submodules/halo-ce-universal/port/linux/game/tag_schema_models.c#L862)). Four-way aiming interpolation obtains four frame pointers together ([`model_animations.c:1867-1883`](../../submodules/halo-ce-universal/source/models/model_animations.c#L1867)). The same source audit classifies animation data as relevant beyond draw: object/unit and first-person poses, movement/root motion, melee metadata, devices/vehicles, sky/camera paths. A report of total bytes “accessed” or max single record is not a simultaneous pin set.

Halo-64 is only a seam precedent: its source branches the public frame accessor under `HALO_N64`, puts frames in 4 KiB cartridge chunks and a 16-entry 64 KiB LRU (`submodules/halo-64/port/n64/src/n64_anim.c:4-12,22-23,108-177`). That uses cartridge DMA and N64 asset conversion. It does not establish safe eviction for HCU's four simultaneous pointers, PS2 FileXio/DVD/OPL latency, CE compressed-validation semantics, or PS2 EE cache size. Do not copy the 64 KiB value, N64 storage rules, or N64 product cuts.

## Feasibility decision: one safe slice versus a capacity claim

The d20 lower bound blocks a *guest-side resident d20 observer run today*: the current whole-image tag+BSP data, claims, ELF/BSS, CE state and system allocation already exceed the committed budget. A host fixture with oversized buffers can exercise parsers/transactions, but it cannot be reported as PS2 placement/high-water or campaign runtime behavior. The Step 7/9 host transaction results and UI-only PCSX2 peaks are useful lower-level correctness evidence; neither supplies actor/script/render high-water for d20.

Therefore the immediate feasible step is limited to a **diagnostic-only access/lifetime observer**, not a cache or representation change:

1. Freeze one new evidence tuple first: dirty-tree diffs/hashes for parent, HCU, halo-ps2 and Halo-64; exact production closure/rooted object/combined ELF and EE flags; schema ELF; same-corpus map and package hashes; converter and instrumentation diff; budget/memory-map hashes; and PCSX2/toolchain settings. Do not reuse the static `2fba…` report join as runtime evidence.
2. Add a profile-gated, fixed-size event/counter layer with no allocation, I/O, scheduling, or pointer mutation in the default build. Hook the schema validator's animation-data reads, the public frame accessor, all three compressed bypasses, default-data, indexed and bulk frame-info access, and BSP/data access candidates selected by the complete source inventory. Assign each event an explicit callsite/phase and a stable record identity; do not retain an unleased raw pointer in the log.
3. First validate hook coverage and *observational equivalence* with host ILP32 fixtures and the normal supported UI guest: instrumentation off/on must preserve validator corrections, decoded pose output, arena cursors, CE global pointers, and gameplay hashes wherever a deterministic fixture exists; event-buffer overflow must fail the diagnostic run, not silently drop observations. UI results prove hook plumbing only.
4. For a resident-oracle workload, use a host-only overprovisioned execution only if it can run the actual HCU CE tick and authored campaign scenario against the exact resident tag/BSP data. Label it strictly as source-access/locality evidence. A parser, schema walker, static range audit, synthetic animation calls, or oversized host BSP transaction is not a representative campaign live set. If no such whole-game host execution exists, stop after observer validation and record the d20 runtime live set as unavailable; do not substitute an incomplete workload.
5. Record separately: whole-map bytes, event/call counts, unique touched ranges, nesting/overlap intervals, maximum concurrent logical data held by call scopes, worst per-tick requests/bytes, and provider latency (if any). Since the observer does not page data, it measures reuse/locality, not memory savings or a cache's required capacity. Use it to decide whether a lossless typed-lease prototype is worth a separate design review.
6. Before any lease, BSP-field externalization, or owner sharing is proposed, require a second source-backed topology review covering *all* EE owners, every pointer escape/validation path, physical destination, cache+metadata+staging, failure/unload/retry, actors/players/render/audio, and transition overlap. Only a nonnegative, measured worst-phase ledger with reserve untouched can authorize a prototype. Do not gate implementation on the optimistic animation-only residual as though it were available capacity.

This slice is useful but cannot by itself unblock campaign-map admission or Step 8. If the user-facing objective is the earliest visible/gameplay map rather than an all-map capacity solution, a separate Longest-only vertical slice can be considered as a **diagnostic map path**: its frozen complete tag/B000 pair fits arithmetically in the existing aggregate `current_bsp` span under a proposed pre-boot split, but that split and its all-owner peaks are not yet target-qualified. It must remain a separate candidate and cannot substitute for Step 8's all-campaign gate or change catalog product support. The current Step 8 text and [`m2-capacity-next-slice-2026-10-21.md`](m2-capacity-next-slice-2026-10-21.md) should be reconciled before starting mutually exclusive work: the former says do not begin direct-to-final map integration until a capacity design exists, while the latter recommends Longest implementation as a scoped vertical slice. This review favors the former for Step 8 compliance; if Longest is authorized as a diagnostic experiment, explicitly preserve closed admission and label all results partial.

## Gates that must remain closed

* All ten campaign maps and all stock multiplayer map entries remain `capacity-not-qualified`; no catalog admission based on smallest BSP, compressed package size, host-overprovisioned fixtures, or one successful map.
* No production lease/cache or BSP-vertex subtraction: CE direct pointers, validator publication/correction, collision/material/PVS/sound/surface queries, pointer lifetime, source failure, and render replacement are not yet fully mediated and measured.
* No arena enlargement/repartition, aliasing/overlap of tag and BSP, reduction of named owners from nominal zero-startup counters, use of GS/IOP/SPU2 as EE memory, or spending the 5,767,168-byte reserve.
* No M2 exit, gameplay-map capacity/high-water claim, PCSX2 scene-render claim, or target performance claim. A PCSX2 full-scene functional pass would still not prove physical EE cycle or FileXio latency.
* No Halo-64/N64 pool sizes, cache policy, actor/script/effect omissions, or 8 MiB/94 MHz assumptions.
