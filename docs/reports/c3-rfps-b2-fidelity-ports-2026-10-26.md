# C3 FPS research B2: fidelity boundaries and portable optimizations

Date: 2026-10-26  
Scope: source-only audit for pursuing >30 distinct presented frames/s on PS2/PCSX2 while retaining the current intended image quality. This research describes source baseline `320a904` before the later first-person correction work. No code changes, builds, guest runs, or asset-byte extraction were performed for this report. Later first-person reports document implementation changes and must not be read back into the audited baseline. PCSX2 FPS/VPS is not evidence of physical-console speed.

## Finding

“Graphical fidelity” is only partly determined by the renderer. In this port, the final image is the product of (1) CE tag/map content and conversion, (2) CE-owned camera, pose, first-person, animation, and effect state, (3) PS2 visibility, projection, LOD, clipping, material, lighting, and output policy, and (4) resident texture/geometry resource lifetime and scheduling. Fixing CPU cost in any of those layers can change the image even when no GS quality switch changes. A useful fidelity-preserving optimization reuses or batches equivalent work while leaving inputs, selected content, per-view decisions, and emitted pixels unchanged.

Current source already enables BSP textures and lightmaps, material alpha/blend flags, sky model geometry, model textures, cluster ambient/sun lighting, depth testing, and CE-derived skeletal poses. The architecture specifically separates 30 Hz simulation from a potential 60 Hz render/present cadence; the cadence report explains why immutable two-endpoint ownership and presentation side effects remain unproven. There is no basis here for claiming >30 FPS on a console or even same-fidelity 60 FPS in PCSX2.

## Observed quality-affecting constraints and likely opportunities

1. **Near-plane clipping has a time-budget compromise.** `render_options_t.clip_budget_ms` defaults to 6 ms. In the BSP build path, after the budget is exceeded, remaining unsafe batches are skipped (`clip_skipped_batches`); these can be partially visible large/near triangles. This is an explicit quality-vs-CPU compromise. First profile the count/cost and target EE clipping overhead (batch ordering, exact AABB classification, cached clip inputs) while preserving the same clipped polygons; an adaptive policy that simply skips more clipping is a quality cut. Acceptance must include difficult near-wall/large-triangle camera paths with no newly omitted polygons versus an unbounded/reference clip capture. (Observed code: `bsp_render.c` around unsafe batch clipping and `clip.c`; no measured current frequency of skips in this audit.)

2. **The BSP VU1 path drops partially visible triangles outside its guard band.** `bsp.vsm` documents that after perspective divide, triangles with any vertex beyond G=7 / near plane are collapsed to zero area; “partially visible large triangles” are dropped rather than clipped. EE clipping is meant to handle risky batches first, but its budget means that guarantee is not universal. This is a fidelity opportunity: correctness-preserving clip coverage may add EE cost, so measure hotspot and optimize the clip/cull pipeline before broadening coverage. Do not change guard band or lower work ceiling without image comparison.

3. **A candidate projection/basis defect is not proven from the standalone path.** The older standalone `fpview_place` feeds camera yaw/pitch to `mat4_from_ypr`, but it is not the active C3 first-person producer/consumer path. Do not infer a C3 bug from that function. C3 uses CE-published camera-local matrices and parent camera composition. The projection uses `ky = kx * aspect`, consistent with the comment that tan(vfov/2)=tan(hfov/2)/aspect; don’t “correct” this by intuition.

4. **The oversized/intersecting weapon was observed in the pre-fix C3 baseline, but later source work identified a renderer transform defect.** The cache A/B runs at the audited baseline retain the defect in both arms, so they remain a valid cache-parity comparison only. Later R1/I1 found missing model-specific inverse-bind application in the active CE-published palette path and implemented that correction; R2/I2 separately added first-person depth/projection/culling isolation. Treat those reports as subsequent fixes, not properties of baseline `320a904`. A speedup claim must still compare against matched corrected output and cannot come from hiding/culling the weapon or changing authored offsets.

5. **Resource residency and batching are fidelity-sensitive.** BSP cache has keys for map textures/lightmap pages and reserved object texture namespace; it respects kick fences before invalidation/eviction. Object cache A/B is a strong exactness example: RGB, canonical snapshots, bitmap request dumps and queue semantics matched, but four host-rate deltas were mixed and tiny (−0.244% to +2.025%; one run/config each). It offers evidence that reuse can preserve pixels, not a reliable FPS win. Cross-view object prep is only portable if invalidation includes publication, map/object generation and resource residency epoch, and if no per-camera products are reused.

## What transfers from halo-ce-universal and Halo-64

### HCU PS2

HCU retains CE game sources as state authority and has an explicit PS2 adapter boundary for platform services and a parent render snapshot. Active C3 first-person fidelity is determined by CE-published gun/hands resource identities and palettes (`hcu_render_objects_select_first_person` / pose callback), followed by parent camera-local composition; standalone `fpview.c` is not this path. The B1 report gives the key cadence limitation: realtime CE scheduler applies fixed 1/30 updates, calls `main_game_render(1/30)` once after all due ticks, then synchronously consumes the snapshot. To present >30 distinct frames without changing gameplay fidelity, separate presentation scheduling from that tick and interpolate immutable, compatible tick snapshots. The active `HCU_C3_FRAME_SNAPSHOT` implementation uses three reader-pinned frame banks; the older v1 double-bank is inactive. A faster renderer must retain both endpoints until interpolation and DMA/GS reads complete, while keeping render-side state updates once-per-tick or otherwise presentation-pure. Follow B1’s ownership and cadence gates before trying 60 Hz.

### Halo-64

The local adaptation report and Halo-64 sources show that N64’s renderer replacement is a stateful adapter, not just no-op draw calls: it prepares per-window camera/frustum/visible clusters, invokes CE first-person update and HUD state updates, then dispatches to a custom renderer. This is transferable as *ownership discipline*: classify every state-advancing CE call separately from drawing, retain CE pose/camera/HUD/effect semantics, and test the render snapshot boundary. Halo-64’s converter/streamed target resources and typed call-closure checks are also reusable ideas, but its RSP/RDP implementation, texture encodings, memory ceilings, particle removal, smaller pools, simulation throttling, and N64 floating-point substitutions are not PS2 optimizations that preserve visual fidelity.

## Explicit quality cuts to reject for this goal

Do not count as fidelity-preserving: disabling BSP lightmap pass or materials/textures; reducing texture/bitmap dimensions or palette precision; dropping sky geometry or alpha/blended materials; raising LOD bias/near-pixel cutoff or lowering object/effect/particle capacities; shortening draw distance; omitting partially visible BSP triangles, objects, or effects; changing output resolution/buffer format/field mode; replacing model lighting with neutral values; reducing animation/pose tick rates without interpolation; copying Halo-64’s no-particle behavior; or tuning first-person offsets to conceal the known weapon baseline issue. Such modes may be useful as diagnostic upper bounds, but cannot satisfy “retain graphical fidelity.”

## Relation to existing performance evidence and reporting rules

The object-sharing A/B report shows exact output with its cache, but no consistent PCSX2 FPS gain (one case +2.025%, the other three near flat or negative; single trial per setting). The cadence B1 report describes a prior workload with 30.0096 sim ticks/s but 14.9648 rendered frames/s, and explicitly distinguishes repeated fields, emulator VPS, presentation count, and distinct game frames. Keep these measurements separate. Future claims should report: distinct render IDs/sec; actual flip intervals and repeated-image count; fixed 30 Hz tick rate and dropped-tick deltas; per-phase EE costs; VIF/GS completion timing; framebuffer mode; cache/clip/chain counters; exact source/config/ELF; and matched image parity. Mark physical PS2 result “not measured” until tested on hardware. No user request or source observation here constitutes such a test.
